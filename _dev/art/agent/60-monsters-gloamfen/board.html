v1 beside v2

Left of each pair, v1 (kept in out_v1/); right, v2. Kikimora: v1 read as a gray funnel, v2 is smaller, thin, with a narrow pale face and a one-line hooked nose. Dullahan: v1's head read as a cream shield, v2 has a round skull head, a dark neck stump and a long horse face.

Jiangshi (regular)

Chinese hopping corpse: a tall black official's hat, a paper talisman down the face, a pale mint face with fangs, a dark jade robe with a gold rank badge, both arms straight out. Second source: The World of Chinese (2014) confirms the stiff hopping corpse, the forehead fu and the tall hat.

Adze (regular, swarm, flier)

Ewe firefly swarm: one big firefly, two small, drifting sparks, glowing abdomens. The firefly only, never the possessed human. Second source read (godsandmonsters.info) says the firefly form is a 1993 Bunson encyclopedia image, not Ewe testimony itself: UNVERIFIED: no second source found; drawn anyway. Living belief: handle with care.

Will-o'-Wisp (regular, floating light, flier)

A floating teal flame with a face and a curling tail. D&D SRD 5.2 monster (CC BY 4.0): the game's credits need the SRD credit.

Kikimora (regular)

Slavic marsh hag: wild moss hair, a long sharp nose, thin arms, bare feet, a spindle. Sources: Wikipedia and the sacred-texts Slavic folklore pages named in the brief. Unsure: the face is the weakest read of the batch.

Draugr (elite)

Norse grave-brute: a swollen death-blue corpse in a rusted horned helm, mail pauldrons, gray beard, belly over a belt, a rusted axe. Second source: Grettir's Saga (Gutenberg 347), per the brief.

Empousa (elite)

Greek spirit: hair of flame, pale fanged face, tattered dress, a bronze leg with a plate foot and a gray donkey leg with a hoof. Second source: History Cooperative (cites Aristophanes' Frogs and Philostratus).

Dullahan (mini-boss, level 55, a rider)

Irish headless rider: a hollow-collared cloaked rider behind a huge black horse's face, his own pale grinning head held out on one arm, a spine whip. Second source: Notre Dame Rare Books page confirms the headless rider with the head under his arm (Croker); no Irish Folklore Commission record exists.
