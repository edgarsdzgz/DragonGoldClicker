Left of each pair, v1 (kept in out_v1/); right, v2. v1 read as a gray robot with a gold shield (Nixie) and a green glove (Hydra).

Kappa (regular, swarm)

Japanese river imp: a teal child with a big water dish brimming on its bowl-cut head, a small pale beak and a brown turtle shell behind the shoulders. A pest, not a deity. Hurt: the dish water spills. Second source: World History Encyclopedia (confirms dish, shell, beak, drowning, and that a spilled dish leaves it helpless).

Kongamato (regular, flier)

Zambian river pterosaur: rust-red body and leathery bone-fingered wings, a tall backward crest and a long beak of white teeth. The walk is a wingbeat; it has a shadow. Cryptid. UNVERIFIED: second source is only a popular bestiary site (abookofcreatures.com), no scholarly one found.

Nixie (regular, ranged)

v2: German water spirit, a slender pale blue-green figure waist-deep in a pool, long wet green hair over the shoulders, gold eyes, a small golden harp slanted against the chest. Hurt: the harp breaks. Two sources in the brief (Dash, Gutenberg).

Jenny Greenteeth (elite, brute)

English river hag: bright emerald skin, a wild tangle of dark hair hung with duckweed, a grin of green teeth and long clawed arms reaching from the water she lurks in. Three sources in the brief.

Ahuizotl (elite, brute)

Aztec water beast: dog-sized, wet spiked black-purple fur, monkey hands, and a tail that rises beside it and ends in a big pale human hand (the mark). Living-belief note: yellow; kept a monster, never a deity. Second source: godsandmonsters.info, which cites the Florentine Codex and Hernandez. Hurt: the tail hand is lost.

Berberoka (mini-boss, brute)

Philippine pond-drainer: a huge mud-and-moss lump with gold eyes on bulbs, reeds on its crown, a wide toothed mouth, and the drained pond's water flooding out in front with stranded fish. UNVERIFIED: the second source (FilipiKnow, a popular article) confirms only the drain-and-drown and the region; no source describes its body, so this design is mine.

Lernaean Hydra (boss)

v2: a low coiled body under five big heads on long curving necks (two arching out, two curling forward, the huge pale-horned deathless head in the middle; the myth names nine, v2 shows five for readability). The necks sway in turn. Hurt: the left arching head is cut off. Second source: Apollodorus, Library 2.5.2 (Perseus). Drawn from Greek myth, no credit needed.
