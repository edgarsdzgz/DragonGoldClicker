# Bestiary: brainstorm and research plan (2026-10-29)

The goal: continue the old game's bestiary pattern across all of its Lands, and fill it with variety from
world folklore, myth, fantasy and open tabletop content. Part 1 is the pattern. Part 2 is a first
brainstorm. Part 3 is how the research is done.

**The lore is canon** (owner, 2026-10-29): this is the same game as the old Draconia Chronicles, made as a
clicker. New monsters fit the old lore and add to it, and never change it. Sources:
`C:\Users\ediaz\Coding_Projects\dragonIdler\docs\archive\v2.3-extended\` (`01_Vision_Lore_World.md`,
`04_Progression_Maps_Wards_Lands.md`, `26_Content_Packs_Clans_Bestiary.md`).

## Part 1: The pattern

From the old docs:

- **The world is Lands, and each Land is split into Wards.** Dragons name each Land for its own geography,
  positive or neutral, never for its foes ("Horizon Steppe", not "Goblin Wasteland").
- **Each Land has an element, a faction and a boss.** The faction is a people or creatures corrupted by
  the Human-Wrought Evil, the force human hubris let in through the rifts. It "corrupts and inverts": it
  turns protection into consumption and fire into wildfire.
- **Each faction has a set roster:** about three minions, two elites and one boss. It also has a lore
  origin (who they were before), a combat signature and a land hazard.
- **Five enemy types run across every Land:** swarm, ranged, shielded, spectral and chitin.
- **The bestiary's drawing rules apply to every addition** (`bestiary.md`): gold eyes; magenta only for the
  Unmaking's cracks; red only for damage; a human enemy must never read as a villager; never crimson hide
  with gold plates (the player's alone).

| 2 | Ember Reaches | fire | Flame-Touched Cultists | Forge-Master of the Inferno | Lore and outline; drawn and in the game |

**How Lands could map onto the clicker's horde levels** (a proposal): one Land per ten horde levels, ending
on its boss. This already fits what is in the game. The Khagan closes level 10 and the Nomads arrive at
levels 7 and 8. The Forge-Master closes level 20 and the cultists arrive at levels 11 to 13. Under this
plan, Mistral Peaks covers levels 21 to 30 and ends on the Tempest Lord, and so on through Shadowmere at
91 to 100. The Corrupted Drake, Corrupted Wyvern and Lich bosses, already drawn, would move to the Lands
they suit (see Part 2).

## Part 2: First brainstorm

These are candidates for research to confirm, not decisions. Each comes from a real tradition. Each would
be drawn as that tradition's monster corrupted by the Human-Wrought Evil, never as its sacred or
benevolent form. The sensitivity levels green, yellow and red are explained in Part 3.

### Land 3: Mistral Peaks (lightning, wind): the Storm-Callers
The faction already has its name, theme ("weather control and elemental mastery") and boss, the Tempest
Lord. What was left open: who they were. A proposal that adds to the lore: mountain weather-priests who
tried to calm the storms the Evil brought, and became them.
- **Minions:**
  - a Storm-Caller acolyte (ranged);
  - kamaitachi, sickle-clawed wind weasels (Japan) (swarm);
  - a hail slinger.
- **Elites:**
  - tengu, the mountain goblins of Japan (green; the great tengu lords are revered, so use only the
    common kind);
  - raijū, the lightning beast (Japan).
- **Mini-boss:** the roc (Arabian and Persian tales).
- **Existing art that fits:** the Harpy, the Storm Rider and the Windcaller.

### Land 4: Riverglass Delta (steam): the Drowned
Proposal: river and delta folk the Evil pulled under. A hot, steaming marsh.
- **Minions:**
  - kappa, the river imp (Japan);
  - a Drowned fisher, a masked gray-green human who must not read as a villager;
  - a steam toad.
- **Elites:**
  - Jenny Greenteeth, the river hag (England);
  - the each-uisge, a water horse (Scotland).
- **Mini-boss:** the ahuizotl, a water dog with a hand on its tail (Aztec).
- **Boss:** the nuckelavee (Orkney), a skinless horse-and-rider sea demon.
- **To research:** Mokele-mbembe, the river creature of Congo legend; the ninki nanka, a swamp dragon of
  the Gambia (dragon-kin, so check against the "dragons are few" lore).

### Land 5: Elderbough Crown (mist): the Mist-Bound
Proposal: the old forest's fey, gone feral in the Evil's mist.
- **Minions:**
  - will-o'-the-wisps (England);
  - redcaps (Scottish Borders);
  - huli jing, fox spirits (China).
- **Elites:**
  - jorogumo, the spider woman (Japan);
  - patupaiarehe, the pale fairy folk of the misty hills (Maori; yellow, check with Te Ara and
    guidance from Maori sources).
- **Mini-boss:** the Spider Queen (already drawn).
- **Boss:** a forest lord of the leshy kind (Slavic), corrupted. The name would be ours.

### Land 6: Gloamfen Mire (void): the Gloam-Risen
The dead and the hollow.
- **Minions:**
  - jiangshi, the hopping corpse (China);
  - pishacha, flesh-eating ghouls (India);
  - the Skeleton and the Wraith (already drawn).
- **Fliers:** phi krahang, a flying ghost with winnowing baskets for wings (Thailand; yellow).
- **Elites:**
  - draugr, the barrow-dead (Iceland);
  - vetala, a spirit that rides corpses (India).
- **Mini-boss:** the barghest or Black Shuck (England), next to the Spectral Hound.
- **Boss:** the gashadokuro, a giant skeleton (Japan), or the Lich (already drawn).

### Land 7: Saffron Dunes (plasma): the Glass-Scoured
Desert under a sky the Evil cracked. Avoid dressing this faction as any real people.
- **Minions:**
  - the ghul, the desert ghoul (Arabian tales);
  - girtablilu, the scorpion-men (Mesopotamia);
  - a sand-glass golem.
- **Elites:**
  - the manticore (Persian);
  - the olgoi-khorkhoi, the death worm (Mongolian cryptid).
- **Mini-boss:** the Basilisk (already drawn).
- **Boss:** an ifrit (Arabian tales; yellow, because jinn belong to Islamic belief; use the folkloric
  ifrit of the stories only).

### Land 8: Tideglass Coast (lightning): the Corsair Fleets
Storms over glass beaches.
- **Minions:**
  - the merrow (Ireland);
  - ponaturi, sea goblins that come ashore at night (Maori; yellow);
  - a shipwreck crab.
- **Elites:** the Mantair Corsair (the old game's flier, already drawn); the lusca (Caribbean).
- **Mini-boss:** the kraken (Norway).
- **Boss:** the umibozu, a dark sea giant that rises beside ships (Japan).

### Land 9: Hollowspire Below (ice): the Rime-Bound
Frozen caverns under the mountains.
- **Minions:**
  - knockers, mine spirits (Cornwall);
  - kobolds in their original form, German mine spirits, as cousins of the ones in the game;
  - frost bats.
- **Elites:**
  - yuki-onna, the snow woman (Japan);
  - the grootslang, a cave serpent (South Africa; check sources).
- **Mini-boss:** the Yule Cat (Iceland).
- **Boss:** Gryla, the troll mother (Iceland), or a frost giant of the Norse hrimthursar. The Stone Golem and
  Iron Golem (already drawn) fit as guards.

### Shadowmere: the edge of the Rift
The last Land, where the portals are. It holds the Unmaking's own (wraith, shadow phantom, ember wraith)
and the worst of everything. Its boss waits on the story: who began the Unmaking. Under the old lore that
was human hubris, the heroes of the old world.

## Part 3: The research plan

### 3.1 The rules every candidate must pass

1. **License.**
   - **Green:** myth and folklore, which are in the public domain as ideas. We write our own descriptions.
   - **Green with credit:** the D&D System Reference Document (SRD 5.1 and SRD 5.2), released by Wizards of
     the Coast under CC BY 4.0. A monster may be used if it is in the SRD, with the credit line the SRD
     asks for. Pathfinder content under the ORC license works the same way.
   - **Red:** D&D creatures that are not in the SRD. Wizards of the Coast keeps them as its own (beholders,
     mind flayers, displacer beasts and others). Also red: creatures invented by modern books, films or
     games. Inspiration is fine; names and designs are not.
2. **Sensitivity.**
   - **Green:** folklore monsters widely used in fiction (kappa, draugr, redcap).
   - **Yellow:** creatures from a living belief or a culture's sacred tradition. They can be used with
     care: drawn as a corrupted creature of the story, never mocked, with the source and the reason noted.
     Examples: krasue, phi krahang, patupaiarehe, ifrit.
   - **Red, never used as an enemy:**
     - deities and their sacred animals or mounts;
     - sacred guardians, such as taniwha, which many iwi regard as kaitiaki;
     - beings that Indigenous communities ask outsiders not to use, such as the wendigo and the skinwalker.
   - **No faction is dressed as a real ethnic group.**
3. **Fit.** It has to work as an enemy marching up a road at about 16x20 pixels, with a clear silhouette.
   It must not read as a villager, and it must fit a Land and an enemy type.
4. **Two sources,** at least one of them more solid than a wiki.

### 3.2 Where to look

| Kind | Sources |
|---|---|
| Maps (where to start; never the only source) | Wikipedia's "List of legendary creatures" pages, by type and by country; its folklore categories |
| Public domain collections | Project Gutenberg, the Internet Archive, sacred-texts.com: Jacobs' *English Fairy Tales*; Keightley's *The Fairy Mythology*; Lang's fairy books; Jon Arnason's *Icelandic Legends* (translated); Grey's *Polynesian Mythology*; translations of the *Shan Hai Jing* |
| Folklore scholarship | D. L. Ashliman's *Folklore and Mythology Electronic Texts* (University of Pittsburgh); university folklore archives; Encyclopaedia Britannica |
| By region | Theoi Project (Greek); Te Ara, the Encyclopedia of New Zealand (Maori, with cultural context); Japanese yokai references (yokai.com is a secondary source, for facts only); national museum and heritage sites for Thailand, India, Kenya and Iceland |
| Tabletop | The official SRD 5.2 and 5.1 documents; the Open5e API (`api.open5e.com`), which lists SRD monsters as data and records which document each comes from |

Agents write descriptions in their own words and quote nothing longer than a short name or phrase. They
look at pictures for ideas only, never to copy them, as the art brief says.

### 3.3 What each candidate gets: one row in a catalog

The catalog lives in `_dev/docs/bestiary-research/catalog.csv`, with one row per creature:

| Field | Example |
|---|---|
| name, other names | Kappa |
| origin (culture, country, region) | Japan |
| source type | folklore, myth, cryptid, SRD, literature |
| sources | two or more URLs, with the date each was read |
| looks (our words) | turtle shell, beak, a water-filled dish on its head |
| behavior (our words) | drags people into rivers; bound by courtesy |
| Land fit, element | Riverglass Delta, steam |
| enemy type | swarm, ranged, shielded, spectral, chitin, flier |
| rank fit | regular, elite, mini-boss, boss, event |
| license | green, green with credit, red, with the reason |
| sensitivity | green, yellow or red, with the reason |
| reads at 16x20 | yes or no, with a note |
| already in the bestiary? | none, or the one it repeats |

### 3.4 How it runs

1. **Gathering.** Five researcher agents on **Sonnet**, because judging license and sensitivity needs more
   than **Haiku**. Two run at a time. Each covers two regions and adds 25 to 40 rows:
   - East Asia: Japan, China, Korea;
   - Southeast and South Asia: Thailand, the Philippines, Indonesia, India;
   - Europe: England, Ireland, Scotland, the Nordic lands with Iceland, the Germanic and Slavic lands;
   - Africa and the Middle East: Kenya and East Africa, West and Southern Africa, Arabian, Persian,
     Mesopotamian, Egyptian;
   - Oceania, the Americas and Greece: New Zealand and the Pacific, Mesoamerica and South America, Greek.
2. **The tabletop pull.** One agent on **Haiku**: list the SRD monsters from Open5e with their source
   document. This is a mechanical list job.
3. **Fitting.** One **Sonnet** agent removes duplicates and assigns each Land's roster: minions, elites,
   mini-boss and boss. It checks every pick against the drawing rules and reports gaps.
4. **The owner picks.** I build a board: one card per candidate, grouped by Land, with sources and
   sensitivity shown. The owner keeps, cuts or moves each one.
5. **Into the bestiary.** The picks go into `bestiary.md` as candidates. Then art batches follow, as
   before: pilot first, the owner approves, then the rest.

**Cost:** 7 agents in all (6 Sonnet, 1 Haiku), at most 2 at once. Nothing launches until the owner says
go.

### 3.5 Decided (owner, 2026-10-29: "Let's do as you recommend")

1. One Land covers ten horde levels and ends on its boss (Part 1).
2. The Storm-Callers were mountain weather-priests who tried to calm the Evil's storms and became them.
3. The regions are as listed in 3.4.
4. Event monsters (the nian, the Yule Cat, the Yule Lads and any others found) come as seasonal visitors,
   not as Land monsters.

The research started the same day. The shared brief for the agents is `bestiary-research/BRIEF.md`.
